Research note · Urban theory · UCL

Why car dependence persists

I connected research on driving attitudes with an account of peri-urban development in Galicia to examine how infrastructure, incentives and social expectations can reinforce each other.

Systems thinking / Scenario framing / Literature synthesis

Infrastructure and driving norms

One study examined how older adults understand driving and independence. Another examined the political economy of dispersed development. I brought these perspectives together: a household’s travel choices are shaped both by the available alternatives and by expectations about what normal mobility looks like.

A proposed research question

I proposed investigating how living in fragmented, low-accessibility places shapes norms around driving. The extension connects material constraints with social expectations, rather than treating attitudes as a separate explanation.

Comparing development scenarios

I contrasted continued dispersed growth with a hypothetical disruption to that development pattern. The useful question was whether changed planning conditions would weaken the reinforcing relationships, and whether some places would remain more constrained than others. These scenarios organised the reasoning rather than predicting future traffic.

Scope of the work

A theoretical synthesis leading to a proposed research question. The scenarios compare possible mechanisms of change, rather than forecast traffic or simulate an intervention.

Source & presentation +

Urban Systems Theory assessment, UCL. The main comparison draws on Piatkowski and Marshall on driving and ageing, Delage and Rousseau on peri-urban development, and Batty on urban complexity.
