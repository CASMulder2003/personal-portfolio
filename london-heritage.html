London Heritage Explorer

In a four-person team, I curated the spatial data and developed mobile navigation for London Heritage Explorer, a prototype walking app. It combines route planning, GPS, arrival detection and audio to introduce heritage sites along a walk.

Users plan a route on a desktop map. On a phone, the app uses local cues, audio and information revealed at arrival to prompt exploration. This separates route selection from the information needed during the walk.

Which features guide the walk?

I sourced and processed the datasets in R. The report compares nine candidate feature types with four retained environmental cues. Heritage destinations remain a separate layer: the places to discover, rather than the cues used along the way.

Route planning and navigation

Planning a route and following it on foot

I developed the desktop-versus-phone flow and mobile functionality. A first routing pass identifies nearby heritage candidates. A second connects selected stops, with cues filtered to the current segment during navigation.

During the walk

Receive information on arrival

GPS, heading and arrival detection support the mobile journey. Manual arrival confirmation offers an alternative. Category-specific audio precedes site information, and the continuous route line is removed.

Coverage, clutter and navigation limits

Filtering limited map clutter, but Wikipedia-linked heritage favoured places already documented online. Fixed stops constrained the routes, and the prototype remained screen-dependent. These choices would need user testing. The project did not measure changes in attention or spatial learning.

My work and the team’s contributions +

I wrote the design document, completed the initial literature review, curated all spatial datasets in R and developed mobile routing, GPS, compass behaviour, arrival detection, audio and the mobile flow. Matilda Nelson developed the concept and UI sketches. Du Zimo developed the React structure, map interface, route-selection skeleton and foundational styling. Yutong Chen led visual design, map styling and final visual testing.

Evidence and limits +

This case draws on the submitted April 2026 design report and source code. It demonstrates implementation and reasoned design decisions. It contains no structured user evaluation or measured effect on engagement. GPS accuracy, missing skip functionality and the effectiveness of the audio cues remain limitations.
