Urban Simulation · UCL · 2026

London rail network resilience

Comparing station importance and the effects of sequential removals.

I analysed a network of 401 London rail stations to compare which stations matter most for connectivity and where successive losses split the network into smaller groups.

Using Python and NetworkX, I compared three measures of station importance, tested weighted variants and simulated sequential removals. The comparisons show how the choice of measure, weights and removal order changes the result.

Sequential station removal

How does removing stations
split the network?

Under betweenness targeting, the sixth removal produces a sharp fall from 375 to 227 stations in the largest group. The vulnerability emerges from the sequence of losses, rather than a fixed ranking of stations considered in isolation.

Network assumptions
and limitations

Network measures and assumptions +

The supplied graph contains 401 stations and 467 connections, including Underground, Overground and DLR stations. It is the coursework network, not a claim about the complete current system.

Degree counts connections, betweenness counts participation in shortest paths and closeness measures network proximity. The weighted variants respectively use incident passenger-flow totals, inverse-flow costs and segment length. The flow totals are not counts of unique passengers using a station; inverse-flow paths are not observed passenger itineraries.

The removal experiment uses unweighted rankings. It does not estimate disruption probabilities, passenger rerouting or congestion.

Original results and sources +

The six centrality measures were reproduced from the original GraphML using the notebook’s exact definitions. All 60 saved top-table values and all 36 rounded rank correlations were checked against the notebook outputs.

The original recorded station-removal sequences are unchanged. The geographic components were calculated for those sequences and checked against all 48 recorded largest-group counts. The random comparison retains only its recorded endpoint.

Source: CASA0002 Urban Simulation, UCL, 2026, Part I: London network resilience.
