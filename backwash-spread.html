Transport investment and regional relocation

A prototype linking transport access, housing costs and household moves.

I developed an agent-based prototype to explore whether stronger transport links could draw people towards established centres or support surrounding areas.

I designed a hypothetical regional network, household relocation rules and feedbacks between population, opportunities and housing costs. The interactive diagram shows those assumptions and connections.

Household behaviour and feedbacks

Proposed household rules
and local feedbacks

I separated the transport intervention from the household response. A shorter connection changes the accessibility calculation, while affordability and a household-specific threshold shape whether relocation is considered.

Population movement then feeds into local opportunities and housing costs. This structure makes it possible to ask which assumptions generate concentration or dispersal, rather than assuming improved transport benefits every place equally.

01 · CONNECTIONS

Travel between nodes

Selected links become easier to traverse. Weighted shortest paths define proximity within the synthetic network.

02 · OPPORTUNITIES

Access to opportunities

A gravity-style rule combines available opportunities with distance and a household’s income-related travel burden.

03 · HOUSEHOLDS

Relocation choices

Voluntary moves depend on an accessibility gain and affordability. A separate pathway represents housing pressure.

04 · FEEDBACK

Local feedback

Population shares alter opportunity stocks and housing costs, changing the conditions faced in the next step.

These are proposed rules. Their effects on population and accessibility have not yet been validated.

Study details

Prototype status
and assumptions

The question behind the architecture +

The design draws on the contrast between “spread” effects, where development extends to surrounding places, and “backwash” effects, where advantages concentrate in established centres.

I brought transport access, differentiated households and housing constraints into one structure so that a connection improvement would not automatically imply an equal benefit for every node or household.

Assumptions that matter +

The network, initial values and behavioural thresholds are synthetic. Opportunities and housing costs respond differently by node tier, which builds structural differences into the model. Those choices would need sensitivity analysis.

The proposed indicators include population shares by tier and a Gini measure of population concentration across nodes. That Gini is not an accessibility-inequality measure.

Model ticks are abstract steps. The network is not a map of a real region or a forecast of the Lelylijn’s effects.

Development status and source +

This entry presents the architecture of an exploratory coursework prototype. It does not present calibrated or validated simulation results. Implementation validation, repeated scenario runs and sensitivity analysis are required before making outcome claims.

The diagram is rebuilt directly from the source’s 25 node declarations and 27 link declarations. Node coordinates and initial values are unchanged. No agent trajectories or scenario outcomes have been generated for this page.

Source: CASA0011 Agent-based Modelling for Spatial Science, UCL, 2026, second summative model and design report.
