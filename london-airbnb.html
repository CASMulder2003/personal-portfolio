Market & policy analysis · UCL · 2025

London Airbnb hosting patterns

Defining and mapping a host segment in London’s June 2025 Airbnb data.

Our four-person team analysed London’s June 2025 Airbnb data to examine which hosts and listings would fall within a policy targeting professional operators.

We defined a host segment using portfolio size and calendar availability, then measured its share of listings and mapped its distribution across boroughs. This estimates the policy’s potential coverage, not how many homes it would release.

Host definition and market share

How large is the
selected host segment?

We used portfolio size and calendar availability as indicators of sustained commercial activity. The final rule required a host to have at least two listings each available for 180 days or more, then counted all listings belonging to those hosts.

That selected 4,443 hosts and 30,547 listings from a cleaned sample of 84,503. Availability is a proxy for commercial intensity, not evidence of bookings.

Borough comparison

Where are these
listings concentrated?

Westminster has the largest number of listings associated with the selected hosts. A percentage answers a different question: how much of a borough’s Airbnb market belongs to that segment?

Switch measures and select a borough to compare absolute scale with local market composition. Both use Airbnb listings, with no assumption that listings equal homes or rental tenancies.

Study details

Definition, coverage
and limitations

What the analysis contributes +

The analysis estimates the scale and geographic coverage of one host-based policy definition. It does not estimate how hosts would respond, how visitor demand would change or how many listings would become long-term homes.

Potential housing gains need to be weighed against effects on accommodation supply and services such as cleaning and property management. These are policy considerations, not measured outcomes of this study.

Assumptions that matter +

The 180-day threshold is an analytical choice. A calendar does not establish actual occupancy, legal compliance or whether a listing could become a long-term home. The analysis estimates which listings a definition selects, not how many homes a policy would release.

The map’s percentage denominator is each borough’s mapped Airbnb listings. It does not measure Airbnb’s share of housing stock.

Sources & authorship +

Airbnb in London, Foundations of Spatial Data Science, UCL. Co-authored by C. Mulder, B. Werneck, Z. Xu and H. M. Chan. Results shown here belong to the group study.

The interactive map reproduces the final report’s filtering and borough aggregation. The percentage view is derived from the same original counts. Inside Airbnb’s data assumptions explain the limitations of calendar and location data.
