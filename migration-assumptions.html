Research note · Urban theory · UCL

Personality, migration and causal assumptions

I compared a longitudinal study of personality and migration with a regional study of personality and social and economic indicators. I used causal diagrams to examine confounding, timing and possible feedbacks.

Critical appraisal / Causal reasoning / Research design

Comparing study designs

A longitudinal study followed personality and later migration. A regional cross-sectional study compared average personality traits with social and economic indicators. I examined their different units of analysis, temporal ordering and treatment of background conditions. Evidence that a characteristic precedes an outcome strengthens an explanation, but does not by itself remove confounding.

Adding life events to the model

I proposed adding adversity as a possible upstream influence on personality. Events such as job loss or housing insecurity could change the traits being treated as predictors. This extension made the causal framing more explicit without claiming that I had estimated those effects.

Defining causal roles

A variable such as housing tenure could be a confounder, mediator or moderator under different explanations. Choosing a control variable therefore requires an argument about how the process works. I connected this problem to urban theories that see people and places as shaping one another.

Scope of the work

Critical appraisal of published studies, supported by conceptual diagrams. The adversity pathway is a proposed extension for further research, rather than an estimated effect.

Source & presentation +

Urban Systems Theory assessment, UCL. Compares Shuttleworth and colleagues with Rentfrow and colleagues, then brings in research on life events and personality. The diagram redraws the proposed extension from the assignment.
