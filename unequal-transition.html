Research note · Race and Racism in the Planetary Crisis · Durham

Climate mobility, cobalt extraction and unequal burdens

I examined the usefulness of Spivak’s planetarity, an ethical framework of shared responsibility, through two contrasting cases: Pacific Island climate mobility and cobalt extraction in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. The comparison asked what the framework explains, and where it needs more practical force.

Framework evaluation / Sustainability reasoning / Critical synthesis

Shared vulnerability, unequal positions

I brought planetarity into dialogue with Robinson’s account of racial capitalism, which links economic accumulation to racialised inequalities. Climate mobility foregrounded whose agency and attachments are recognised. Cobalt extraction foregrounded whose labour and environments carry the burdens of green technologies. Together, the cases challenged an account of environmental responsibility that treats everyone as equally positioned.

Where an ethical framework needs institutions

My conclusion supported planetarity’s challenge to distant, abstract accounts of responsibility while questioning how its principles become actionable. I connected the argument to corporate accountability, labour standards and enforceable commitments. The contribution was to evaluate the framework through the cases, retaining its ethical insight while identifying the gap between recognising a responsibility and securing action.

Scope of the work

A conceptual assessment developed from published cases. It examines the reach and limits of an ethical framework, rather than auditing a supply chain or evaluating a policy intervention.

Source & presentation +

Essay on planetarity, race and the climate crisis, Durham University. Key frameworks: Gayatri Chakravorty Spivak’s planetarity and Cedric Robinson’s racial capitalism. The two cases illuminate different forms of unequal burden; they are not treated as interchangeable policy settings.
