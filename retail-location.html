Retail location analysis · UCL · 2026

Supermarket site selection in Havering

Demand estimates and walking access for two proposed sites in Havering.

I compared two potential supermarket sites in Havering, each at two floor areas, to assess demand and access for nearby residents.

I calibrated a spatial interaction model to estimate how trips would be redistributed between stores, then used the walking network to estimate resident reach. The two measures favour different locations.

Comparing the alternatives

I compared new stores of 280 and 2,800 square metres. Total demand from each origin remains fixed, so the model estimates how trips would be redistributed between stores.

Emerson Park attracts more modelled trips at both sizes. The walking comparison reverses that ordering, favouring the alternative near Elm Park.

Study details

Model assumptions
and scope

Calibration and sensitivity +

I calibrated a production-constrained spatial interaction model across 775 origin areas and 56 existing stores. Exponential distance decay gave slightly better agreement with observed flows than the alternative power specification, though overall fit remained modest.

Increasing the distance-decay coefficient to three times its baseline value preserved the larger Emerson Park option’s advantage. This tests an assumption; it is not a calibrated prediction of a particular fare or fuel-price increase. Brand, prices and financial costs are outside the model.

How the walking figures were constructed +

The original analysis snapped output-area centroids and the two candidate centroids to the walking network. An area’s whole population was counted when its snapped point was within 1,200 metres of network distance from the candidate. This corresponds to 15 minutes at the assumed walking speed.

These are approximate, area-based counts. The original centroid calculation used geographic coordinates, and network snapping introduces further approximation. They do not establish access from every home or observed walking behaviour.

Sources and reconstructed figures +

Source: CASA0002 Urban Simulation, UCL, 2026, Part II: spatial interaction modelling. Store-demand figures come from saved notebook outputs and are rounded to whole trips for display.

The walking map was reconstructed offline from the original cached network, boundaries and population data. Network size, candidate nodes, reached-area counts and population totals match the saved notebook results. No demand model was refitted.
