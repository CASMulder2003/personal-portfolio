Youth non-employment in the Netherlands

Examining local differences and testing the stability of regression results.

I analysed data for 342 Dutch municipalities to examine how local demographic and economic characteristics relate to youth non-employment.

I joined and mapped CBS data, built regression models, checked for overlapping predictors and assessed the influence of individual municipalities. I presented the findings in an article and a technical appendix.

Outcome definition

Measuring youth
non-employment

The outcome is the share of all residents aged 15 to under 27 without paid work. It includes people outside the labour force and students without a job. That makes it a measure of non-employment, broader than official unemployment.

Sensitivity to influential observations

How sensitive is the
regression result?

The baseline model associated a larger 20–25 age group with higher youth non-employment, after accounting for other included characteristics. Removing six influential municipalities changed that coefficient from +1.44 to approximately −0.42 and made it statistically non-significant.

Influential places are not automatically errors. The comparison shows that the initial estimate was sensitive to particular contexts, motivating a model that allowed the relationship to differ with urbanity.

Study details

Regression checks
and interpretation

How I developed the analysis +

I joined municipal statistical and geographic data, explored distributions and compared candidate predictors. Correlation and variance-inflation checks exposed overlapping measures, informing a smaller regression specification. Residual checks and Cook’s distance then tested whether the apparent relationships were stable.

The subsequent urban interaction specification increased reported adjusted R² only modestly, from 0.42 to about 0.44. Its value was a more conditional interpretation, with no claim of out-of-sample predictive accuracy.

What the evidence can support +

Municipal associations cannot identify why an individual is out of work, or establish that demographic composition causes the outcome. Rounded counts create extra imprecision in small places. The original predictors were not fully harmonised for a municipal merger, so the reported coefficients remain exploratory. Spatial dependence between municipalities was also not assessed.
