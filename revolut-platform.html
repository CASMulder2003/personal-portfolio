Research note · Platform Geographies · Durham

Revolut’s platform and geographic expansion

I used Revolut to connect two explanations of business growth: bringing activity into a digital platform, and expanding into places with different institutional conditions. Applying both frameworks made product design and geographic strategy part of the same company case.

Business-model analysis / Institutional context / Critical synthesis

Revolut as a financial platform

I applied Srnicek’s account of platform capitalism to Revolut’s bundle of payments, currency exchange and other financial services. The framework directed my attention to user data, repeated engagement and the incentives to keep financial activity within one app. It organised the published material around how the platform could create value and dependence, alongside its convenience for customers.

Institutions and market access

Harvey’s concept of the spatial fix considers how expansion into new places can sustain growth. I used this second lens to examine the case’s account of Revolut’s Lithuanian presence and European market access. This shifted the analysis from what the app offered to the institutional conditions that enabled it to operate across borders.

Product strategy and geographic expansion

The comparison connects the scale of the interface with the scale of institutions. A digital service can reach customers remotely while remaining organised through particular jurisdictions. My conclusion considered the tension between wider access to financial services and questions about data, competition and oversight.

Scope of the work

A company case developed from published research and company material. The work interprets mechanisms of growth and expansion; it does not estimate their contribution to performance or establish market dominance.

Source & presentation +

The Cashless Catalyst: Exploring Revolut’s Impact on Digital Banking through Platform Capitalism and the Spatial Fix, Platform Geographies, Durham University. Key frameworks: Nick Srnicek’s platform capitalism and David Harvey’s spatial fix. The case concerns the company as discussed in the assignment, rather than its current licence status or valuation.
