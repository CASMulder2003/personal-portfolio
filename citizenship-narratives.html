Research note · Contesting Citizenship · Durham

Citizenship and policy categories

Reading published deportee accounts alongside work on state narratives and passports changed how I framed migration. I moved from an administrative question about managing movement towards the relationships, security and belonging that those categories can obscure.

Source comparison / Reflective reasoning / Qualitative synthesis

Borders beyond the checkpoint

I compared visible enforcement with restrictions embedded in passports and documentation. This extended the idea of a border from a physical crossing to the unequal practical ability to move. The literature helped connect an individual’s legal classification with the possibilities available to them in everyday life.

What a legal outcome leaves unresolved

Published accounts of deportation to Jamaica and Ghana described disrupted family ties, economic insecurity and uncertain belonging. I set these accounts alongside the language of migration management: legal removal could be treated as a completed process while its consequences continued. That comparison changed the question I was asking, from how movement is administered to whose experience is missing from that account.

Scope of the work

A reflective synthesis of published narratives and migration theory. The accounts are other researchers’ material and illustrate experiences rather than represent every migrant.

Source & presentation +

The Narratives and Lost Voices of Citizenship, Durham University. The note follows the essay’s change in perspective and its comparison of state categories, documentation and lived experience.
